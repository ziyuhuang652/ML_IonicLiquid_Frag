\section*{Geometry Optimization Benchmark for MACE-Optimized EMI--BF$_4$}

The neutral EMI--BF$_4$ starting structure used in the wall-collision workflow
was optimized with MACE-medium and MACE-POLAR-1 and compared against the
DFT-optimized and crystallographic geometry metrics reported by Laws et al.
The optimization was deliberately lightweight: a single isolated 24-atom
EMI--BF$_4$ ion pair was relaxed with each foundation model using a force
tolerance of \SI{0.03}{eV\per\text{\AA}}. MACE-medium converged in 138 BFGS
steps with a final force maximum of \SI{0.0239}{eV\per\text{\AA}}, while
MACE-POLAR-1 converged in 45 BFGS steps with a final force maximum of
\SI{0.0264}{eV\per\text{\AA}}.

The most important qualitative result is found in the BF$_4$ anion. The
published DFT geometry reports one strongly elongated B$_1$--F$_4$ distance of
\SI{1.703}{\text{\AA}}, whereas the experimental crystallographic structure has
four similar B--F distances near \SIrange{1.38}{1.40}{\text{\AA}}. Both
MACE-medium and MACE-POLAR-1 relax the anion toward a chemically reasonable
near-tetrahedral geometry, with all four B--F distances between approximately
\SIrange{1.40}{1.44}{\text{\AA}}. Therefore, while the MACE foundation models
do not reproduce the asymmetric DFT-optimized BF$_4$ distortion, they are much
closer to crystallography for the anion bond-length substructure, especially
for the anomalous B$_1$--F$_4$ coordinate. Across all tabulated bond lengths,
the mean absolute error relative to the Laws DFT values is
\SI{0.0797}{\text{\AA}} for MACE-medium and \SI{0.0733}{\text{\AA}} for
MACE-POLAR-1; across all tabulated angles, the corresponding mean absolute
errors are \SI{7.19}{\degree} and \SI{6.86}{\degree}. The similarity of these
aggregate errors reflects the tension between matching the reported DFT
distortion and matching the crystallographic BF$_4$ geometry. For reactive
fragmentation workflows, this is a useful sanity check: the MACE models
preserve chemically reasonable local bonding while providing a rapid starting
point for future DFT-informed fine-tuning.
